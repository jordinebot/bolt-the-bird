Last year I went to [Relic Fest](https://getpaird.io/relicfest?lang=en) in Toulouse and played my first Modern tournament after many years away from competitive Magic. I brought a [Budget Dino Whack](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/articles/budget-magic-100-dino-whack-modern) deck and had pretty modest expectations: learn, have fun and, with a bit of luck, win a game or two.

I went 1-7 and finished 134th out of 144 players. But I had a great time and was eager to come back.

So this year I returned to Relic Fest to play the Modern Main Event, which took place yesterday, Sunday, November 1. And things went rather differently!

## Saturday

The main reason I got excited about travelling to Toulouse last year to play in a tournament for a format I had never seriously played was the official playmat illustrated by Rebecca Guay. This year I didn't know the artist ([Donato Giancola](https://donatoarts.com/)) and, to be honest, I wasn't that keen on the illustration (it's the cover of this post). But since we were there, I thought buying the playmat and asking the artist to sign it would make a nice souvenir. That's what we did on the first day, and I have to say Mr. Giancola was incredibly kind!

## Sunday

This time I entered the tournament with [Domain Zoo](https://mtgdecks.net/Modern/domain-aggro). It's an archetype I really like (I also play a version in Pauper), and multicolour decks are generally my favourites. It's obviously much more competitive than last year's deck: it combines very efficient creatures with plenty of interaction and can have explosive starts thanks to the combo of {{< cardname "Leyline of the Guildpact|MKM" >}} and {{< cardname "Scion of Draco|MH2" >}}.

{{< combo "Leyline of the Guildpact" "Scion of Draco" "Territorial Kavu" "Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer|MH2" "Phelia, Exhuberant Shepherd" >}}

The idea is simple: once we have Domain, we can cast Scion of Draco for just {{< mana "2" >}}. It gives our creatures several abilities, such as _hexproof_, _lifelink_, _vigilance_, _trample_ or _first strike_ (or all of them if we have {{< cardname "Leyline of the Guildpact|MKM" >}}!). On top of that, {{< cardname "Territorial Kavu|MH2" >}} becomes a 5/5 for only {{< mana "{R}{G}" >}}.

The deck also has a more interactive game plan, with {{< cardname "Lightning Bolt|REV" >}}, {{< cardname "Leyline Binding|dmu" >}} and creatures like {{< cardname "Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd|MH3" >}} letting me answer my opponent's threats while building my own board.

{{< deck id="domain-zoo-relic-fest-2026" >}}

I spent a long time deciding which version of Domain Zoo to play. Lately the more aggressive version seems to be taking over, closer to the deck's original build, with {{< cardname "Wild Nacatl" >}} and {{< cardname "Tribal Flames" >}}. I was also tempted by the version with {{< cardname "Psychic Frog" >}}. But given how things went, I'm very glad I chose the Phelia version!

Let the tournament begin!

My first opponent is on Grixis Reanimator, with creatures like {{< cardname "Emperor of Bones|mh3" >}}, {{< cardname "Abhorrent Oculus|dsk" >}} and {{< cardname "Archon of Cruelty|mh2" >}}.

#### Game 1

He goes first. I mulligan to six and he goes to five. Despite the initial card advantage, the game is very close. We both have plenty of answers to each other's threats and spend a long time trading resources.

I use Lightning Bolt to kill an Emperor of Bones and several Leyline Bindings to exile another Emperor, an Abhorrent Oculus and even an Archon of Cruelty that he reanimates with {{< cardname "Persist" >}}.

It's a long game with plenty of back and forth, but I eventually come out on top. First game won!

{{< gallery match="relic26-r1*" >}}

#### Game 2

For sideboarding I take out all three Phelias and two {{< cardname "Consign to Memory|mh3" >}}. They have some uses in this matchup, but I wasn't very impressed with them in game one. I bring in two {{< cardname "Containment Priest|m21" >}}, one {{< cardname "Rest in Peace|big" >}} and two {{< cardname "Mystical Dispute|eld" >}}. Honestly, I'm not sure it was the right call, but it worked out...

This time I don't need to mulligan. He opens with {{< cardname "Thoughtseize" >}}, but I already have Leyline of the Guildpact on the battlefield.

I try to play patiently and not rush. The game lasts a few turns, but this time I feel in control almost the whole way. I keep his threats in check and a {{< cardname "Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer|MH2" >}} connects two or three times.

I take the second game, too. Starting the tournament 2-0 feels fantastic, especially after last year's result!

{{< match round="2" player1="Jordi" deck1="Domain Zoo" deck1Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/2359" player2="leobenamor" deck2="Goblins (Snoop Combo)" deck2Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/3209" result="2-1" >}}

My second opponent is on Jund Goblins, with the combo of {{< cardname "Conspicuous Snoop|m21" >}}, {{< cardname "Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker|chk" >}} and {{< cardname "Sling-Gang Lieutenant" >}}. I was surprised to see the deck at the tournament, but I knew it because [LVD](https://youtu.be/iKTOE1s7AOg?si=bjYlOEsIfz2evI0H) played it on Arena last week.

{{< combo "Munitions Expert" "Conspicuous Snoop" "Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker" "Sling-Gang Lieutenant" >}}

#### Game 1

He goes first. I mulligan to six and keep a hand with Leyline of the Guildpact, Ragavan and a {{< cardname "Thundering Falls" >}}, but only one land.

It's not ideal, but I figure that if Ragavan connects and I draw a second land soon, I can get off to a good start. Unfortunately, my opponent kills Ragavan with {{< cardname "Munitions Expert" >}} before it can attack, and I don't find my second land until turn four.

He doesn't put on much pressure, but keeps adding Goblins to the board, including {{< cardname "Taster of Wares|ECL" >}}, a 3/2 Goblin that lets him reveal cards from my hand and exile one.

Toward the end I think I might still stabilize: I have two 5/5 Territorial Kavus, while his creatures are much smaller. But I'm low on life and he has far more Goblins than I have blockers. He eventually attacks for lethal.

#### Game 2

This time the tables turn. My opponent mulligans and keeps a one-land hand.

Now he's the one with mana problems. He can't get his game plan going, and I take advantage to win without much trouble.

The match is tied.

#### Game 3

He goes first. I keep seven with Leyline of the Guildpact and Scion of Draco.

On turn two I play a 4/4 Draco with _flying_, _hexproof_, _vigilance_, _lifelink_, _first strike_ and _trample_. A nightmare for a deck trying to win with small creatures (or an infinite combo that my opponent never manages to assemble).

My opponent desperately looks for an answer and finds one with {{< cardname "Masked Vandal|KHM" >}}, exiling my Leyline and leaving Draco vulnerable. But the damage is done. Draco has connected about three times and I've gained a lot of life. With no clear way to get past it, my opponent concedes at two life.

Second round won! I'm 2-0 in matches.

{{< match round="3" player1="Jordi" deck1="Domain Zoo" deck1Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/2359" player2="DreekC" deck2="Domain Zoo" deck2Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/3935" result="2-1" >}}

Round three is the mirror match against another Domain Zoo deck.

His version has some interesting differences. He plays {{< cardname "Spara's Headquarters|snc" >}} as his triome, runs just one basic Mountain, and includes {{< cardname "Psychic Frog|mh3" >}} and one Emperor of Bones (I found out the exact card count later, of course).

{{< combo "Scion of Draco" "Emperor of Bones" "Psychic Frog" >}}

#### Game 1

He goes first. I mulligan to six, if I remember correctly.

Neither of us has Leyline of the Guildpact to start, and the game is fairly even. But he takes the initiative and, despite my attempts to fight back, wins convincingly.

#### Game 2

I make some sideboard changes, taking out cards like Lightning Bolt, Ragavan and Stubborn Denial and bringing in {{< cardname "Wear/Tear" >}} and {{< cardname "Mystical Dispute" >}}. One curious detail: my opponent had a sideboard guide that he checked after each game to decide what to bring in and take out. Not a bad idea, especially in a format with as many different archetypes as Modern. I wonder if it was the guide Pleyboy shared a few days ago on the Domain Zoo Discord...

The second game is much more interesting. My opponent is in control for much of it and manages to play {{< cardname "Leyline of the Guildpact" >}} on turn four. He also has a Scion of Draco, fully equipped as a result.

I have Domain too, but no Leyline. My Draco is just a flying 4/4 and can't profitably block his.

Little by little, though, I build a stronger board. I play a second Draco and a {{< cardname "Quantum Riddler|eoe" >}}.

Then I finally draw and play my own Leyline! The situation changes completely. My creatures gain all the abilities from Draco, and I go from defending to having a clearly superior board. My opponent sees it and concedes.

#### Game 3

I adjust my sideboard again for the third game, bringing back some of the cards I had taken out.

The game is close again. We both play two Territorial Kavus, but there's an important difference: my opponent gets stuck on two lands and Domain 4. His Kavus are 4/4s while mine are 5/5s. It may not sound like much, but in a mirror match it's huge.

I get Phelia and Quantum Riddler onto the battlefield. At a decisive moment I cast Riddler with _warp_ and give it _haste_ with {{< cardname "Arena of Glory|mh3" >}}.

I attack with everything and use Phelia's ability to temporarily exile my opponent's Psychic Frog, removing one of his blockers.

The attack is enough to win.

After the match, DreekC asks what I think of my Phelia version of Zoo. It feels strange to be asked, since I'm nowhere near an expert in Modern or this deck, but I tell him it's been working well so far and that I've liked {{< cardname "Phelia, Exhuberant Shepherd" >}} more and more for the versatility it brings.

Three rounds, three wins. Things are getting serious!

{{< match round="4" player1="Jordi" deck1="Domain Zoo" deck1Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/2359" player2="Thomas Manso" deck2="Esper Blink" deck2Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/3726" result="0-2" >}}

In round four I face Thomas, who is playing Esper Blink, one of Domain Zoo's [worst matchups](https://mtgdecks.net/Modern/winrates). He's one of those people whose experience is obvious just from the way he shuffles and handles his cards. Naturally, he has also made it to round four with three wins, so this will be a tough one.

{{< combo "Thoughtseize" "Teferi, Time Raveler" "Emperor of Bones" >}}

#### Game 1

I think I mulligan to six. Either way, I keep what looks like an excellent hand: {{< cardname "Leyline of the Guildpact" >}}, three lands, {{< cardname "Quantum Riddler" >}} and {{< cardname "Scion of Draco" >}}.

The problem is that he goes first. On turn one he casts {{< cardname "Thoughtseize|lrw" >}} and takes Draco. On turn two, another Thoughtseize leaves me without Riddler either. Suddenly that promising hand has very little to do.

To make matters worse, he also plays {{< cardname "Teferi, Time Raveler|war" >}}, which severely limits my ability to interact on his turn. I have cards like Lightning Bolt and Consign to Memory, but with Teferi on the battlefield I can't use them the way I'd like. In fact, Teferi leads me to make a costly mistake. My opponent has an Emperor of Bones with one of my Quantum Riddlers exiled under it. I try to kill the Emperor with Lightning Bolt in my main phase, forgetting that he can respond by activating _adapt 2_.

Of course, that's exactly what he does. The Emperor becomes a 4/4, survives the Bolt, and my attempt to remove it wastes a card.

I try to hang on and find a way to regain the initiative, but there's nothing I can do.

#### Game 2

The second game isn't much different. I mulligan again, and Thomas controls the game very confidently. Another {{< cardname "Thoughtseize|lrw" >}}, a {{< cardname "Fatal Push" >}} for my Kavu, and a {{< cardname "Prismatic Ending" >}}...

My first loss of the tournament. My opponent played very well, and I had almost no real chance to win. A good lesson in the gap between knowing the cards in a format and really knowing how to play it.

Still, at 3-1, I'm very happy with the result!

{{< match round="5" player1="Jordi" deck1="Domain Zoo" deck1Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/2359" player2="Aurore" deck2="Monogreen Tron - Eldrazi" deck2Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/3269" result="2-1" >}}

In round five I play Aurore, who's on Tron-Eldrazi. I'm not entirely sure which exact version.

{{< combo "Thought-Knot Seer" "Devourer of Destiny" "Ugin, Eye of the Storms" >}}

#### Game 1

I'm on the play.

I lead with Ragavan on turn one, but Aurore kills it with {{< cardname "Dismember|nph" >}}. I don't remember all the details of this game. We trade resources several times, and I eventually come out ahead.

#### Game 2

I keep a one-land hand. I start with Leyline of the Guildpact and manage to play Scion of Draco. It's a good start, but I struggle to find lands and can't develop at the pace I want. She destroys the Leyline with {{< cardname "Ugin, Eye of the Storms" >}}, taking away Draco's _hexproof_, and then removes it.

Things get complicated from there, and I lose.

#### Game 3

For sideboarding I had brought in the third and fourth {{< cardname "Consign to Memory" >}} and two {{< cardname "Obsidian Charmaw|mh2" >}}.

I keep a slightly strange hand. I don't have Leyline or guaranteed Domain on turn two, but I do have Obsidian Charmaw and three lands (a fetchland, Mountain and {{< cardname "Arena of Glory" >}}). I keep it because I have a clear plan: if Aurore plays the nonbasic lands I expect, I can cast Charmaw on turn three and give it _haste_ with Arena of Glory.

And that's exactly what happens!

Obsidian Charmaw enters the battlefield and destroys one of her lands. Surprisingly, she doesn't find {{< cardname "Dismember" >}} or {{< cardname "Ugin, Eye of the Storms" >}} to remove it, so I can keep attacking and applying pressure.

To top it off, I draw both of my Phelias during the game. I attack with them whenever I can, using their ability to blink Obsidian Charmaw and destroy another of her lands. A few times I know my Phelia will die in combat, but I don't mind: Charmaw's effect is well worth the sacrifice.

I end up destroying three or four lands. With no mana to develop her game plan, Aurore has no way back, and I win.

A very unusual game, where a sideboard card combined with Phelia proved more decisive than the deck's usual threats.

4-1!

{{< match round="6" player1="Jordi" deck1="Domain Zoo" deck1Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/2359" player2="yannickgratacos" deck2="Simic Ritual" deck2Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/3705" result="2-0" >}}

In round six I play Yannick, who's on Simic Ritual, a deck I faced last year too.

Back then I lost 1-2, and one of the cards that caused me the most trouble was {{< cardname "Harbinger of the Seas|MH3" >}}, which turns all nonbasic lands into Islands.

{{< combo "Harbinger of the Seas" "Theorist's Proxy" "Birthing Ritual" >}}

#### Game 1

The game begins with a decent hand, though nothing explosive.

Yannick plays {{< cardname "Harbinger of the Seas" >}}. Last year that creature was a death sentence for my deck, and this year it could be a serious problem too, given my Domain Zoo mana base has 20 nonbasic lands and only two basics.

Fortunately, I can flash in Phelia in response.

On my next turn I attack with Phelia and blink the Harbinger. While it's exiled, my lands regain their abilities, and I can sacrifice a fetchland to find a basic land. I repeat this on the following turn, finding a basic Mountain and Plains. That secures the colours I need to keep playing and even lets me remove the Harbinger with {{< cardname "Prismatic Ending|SOA" >}}.

From there I can develop my board, and Yannick concedes.

#### Game 2

I have a hand with Leyline of the Guildpact and Scion of Draco. One of those hands that looks like it can win all by itself.

Although I could play Draco on turn two, I decide not to rush, keeping {{< cardname "Stubborn Denial" >}} up and waiting until turn three.

When I finally play it, Yannick assesses the situation and concedes immediately, still at 20 life. He has no answer to the creature.

That was a quick win!

5-1. One round to go!

{{< match round="7" player1="Jordi" deck1="Domain Zoo" deck1Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/2359" player2="Martino Bidese" deck2="Rakdos Midrange" deck2Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/3695" result="2-1" >}}

I play the final round against a very nice Italian guy named Martino. He's on a Rakdos Midrange list with cards like {{< cardname "Nethergoyf|MH3" >}}, {{< cardname "Fear of Missing Out|DSK" >}} and some of the new cards from Reality Fracture.

{{< combo "Nethergoyf" "Fear of Missing Out" "Dark Matter Manipulator" >}}

#### Game 1

We both mulligan: I go to six and he goes to five.

My opponent develops his creatures and I answer with Lightning Bolt and Leyline Binding, keeping his board under control.

Meanwhile, I chip away at his life total. It also helps that he deals quite a bit of damage to himself with fetchlands, shocklands and a {{< cardname "Thoughtseize" >}}.

Between his own damage and my attacks, I win the first game fairly easily, finishing at 20 life.

#### Game 2

Before sideboarding, Martino asks if I'm okay with him calling a judge to clarify an interaction he's unsure about. I say of course, and he gets up to speak to the judge. I only bring in one {{< cardname "Rest in Peace|BIG" >}}.

The second game is much closer and more painful. He plays a new Reality Fracture card I knew from Arena drafts: {{< cardname "Dark Matter Manipulator|fra" >}}, a one-mana 1/2 black creature that mills three cards when it enters the battlefield and gets +2/+0 for every seven cards in its controller's graveyard.

The creature slowly chips away at me. Thanks to my {{< cardname "Rest in Peace|BIG" >}} on the battlefield, it stays a 1/2 throughout the game, and I hesitate several times over whether to spend a Bolt on such an apparently harmless creature. I try to answer by attacking with Phelia, even though I don't have any especially useful permanents to blink.

The game drags on as we both look for a way to break the stalemate. We remove each other's threats, except for the 1/2. Looking back at my notes of my life total, I see the sequence 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 2... and realize I should have fired the {{< cardname "Lightning Bolt|REV" >}} I had in hand.

Just when it seems I might still have a chance to win, he finishes me off with a Lightning Bolt.

It all comes down to game three!

#### Game 3

We both mulligan to five and joke that starting with five doesn't feel so bad when we're both in the same boat. The game starts with very few resources and turns into a close battle, with us constantly trading creatures and answers.

He gets stuck on two lands for a few turns and I don't have Domain yet, but I cast two Leyline Bindings for {{< mana "{1}{W}" >}} to exile a {{< cardname "Fear of Missing Out" >}} and a {{< cardname "Moonshadow" >}}.

At one point I have Phelia and {{< cardname "Teferi, Time Raveler|war" >}} on the battlefield. My opponent has a {{< cardname "Moonshadow" >}} that I want to remove with {{< cardname "Prismatic Ending|SOA" >}}. He responds with {{< cardname "Flare of Malice" >}}, sacrificing Moonshadow to remove my Teferi. We resolve the play, I attack with Phelia, and pass the turn. He draws a card and finally plays his third land. That's when I see my Teferi in the graveyard and realize my mistake: with {{< cardname "Teferi, Time Raveler|war" >}} on the battlefield, my opponent couldn't cast an instant in response to Prismatic Ending!

We call a judge. Since the game has moved on and my opponent has drawn a card, fixing it isn't as simple as undoing the last action.

The judges eventually decide to perform a rollback. One of the five cards in my opponent's hand is chosen at random and put on top of his library. We return to the moment Prismatic Ending was on the stack, before the illegal response.

We both receive warnings: he for casting an instant when he couldn't, and me for failing to maintain the game state properly and allowing the error to happen.

But the game goes on.

Martino, who had some mana trouble in the early turns, manages to play a {{< cardname "Blood Moon|mma" >}}.

Fortunately, I have a basic Plains, which lets me keep casting Phelia. I use her ability to exile Blood Moon temporarily several times, restoring access to the colours of mana I need, at least during my second main phase each turn.

Then comes one of the most interesting plays of the whole tournament.

I attack with Phelia and blink Blood Moon. At the beginning of the end step, the delayed trigger that returns Blood Moon to the battlefield goes on the stack. I respond with {{< cardname "Consign to Memory|mh3" >}}, countering that trigger and keeping Blood Moon exiled for good. Martino isn't sure the play works, so we call a judge.

{{< gallery match="relic26-r7*" >}}

The first judge to arrive initially agrees with him. But I'm convinced it works: Blood Moon's return is a delayed triggered ability that goes on the stack, and Consign to Memory can respond to it. I insist, and we ask the head judge to confirm.

The head judge confirms that the play is legal and that Consign to Memory can counter Blood Moon's return.

Fantastic! I've got rid of one of the cards making the game most difficult. But there's another problem: time.

The round's time limit has already passed, and the judge tells us we have only three additional turns to decide the winner. If we can't finish the game, the match will be a draw.

Martino has very few resources. For the last few turns he's had a {{< cardname "Nethergoyf" >}} and a {{< cardname "Fear of Missing Out" >}} on the board, while I've been growing Phelia by blinking my own Rest in Peace. His creatures have been blocking my attacks, though, and I can't close out the game.

We reach the final turn.

My opponent has only a {{< cardname "Fear of Missing Out" >}} on the battlefield, and I have a 4/4 Phelia. He's at six life.

At the end of his turn I surveil and see a Lightning Bolt on top of my library.

I hesitate for a moment, but decide to leave it there.

I draw Lightning Bolt. I attack with Phelia and use her ability to exile my opponent's Fear of Missing Out.

With no blockers, Phelia connects and takes him down to two.

Then I cast Lightning Bolt.

**Win!**

It's almost poetic: he won game two with a direct Bolt, and now I return the favour on the last possible turn of the final game of the tournament!

Martino congratulates me and, very sportingly, tells me he would have conceded at the end because I was clearly winning and a draw wouldn't have helped either of us.

Another guy watching beside us also points out that I could have blinked my Leyline of the Guildpact to give it a later timestamp than Blood Moon, a simpler way to remove its effect. I knew about that interaction, but it never crossed my mind!

## Conclusion

I finish the tournament with **six wins and one loss**, a **12-6 game record**, and [fourth place](https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/live) in the final standings!

Honestly, I never expected this.

Last year I finished 134th out of 144 players, with one win and seven losses. This year I won six of my seven matches.

The deck is obviously a big part of the difference. Last year's Budget Dino Whack was fun and could catch an opponent off guard, but it didn't have the tools to compete consistently against the format's best decks.

Domain Zoo is a whole different story.

The Leyline of the Guildpact and Scion of Draco combination can practically win games on its own, but I also found that the deck has plenty of other ways to come out on top. Territorial Kavu is a very efficient creature, Leyline Binding let me answer all kinds of threats, and Phelia was probably one of the cards that brought me the most joy during the tournament.

Special mention goes to the Obsidian Charmaws in the sideboard. The win against Eldrazi, destroying lands one after another with Phelia's help, was one of the most satisfying games of the day.

I learned a lot, too. The loss to Thomas reminded me that I still have plenty of room to improve, especially against experienced opponents. And my Teferi mistake in the last round shows how easy it is to miss an important interaction, even when you seem to have the game under control.

But I also take away a very positive experience from that final round. Correctly identifying the interaction between Phelia and Consign to Memory and making my case to the judges, who ultimately agreed with me, makes me feel I'm getting to know the deck's subtleties better.

And above all, I'll remember that last Lightning Bolt!

I can't think of a much better way to end the tournament: finding exactly the card I needed on the last available turn and earning a win that left me 6-1 and in fourth place overall.

**From almost last to fourth place in just one year.**
