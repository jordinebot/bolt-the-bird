Last year I went to [Relic Fest](https://getpaird.io/relicfest?lang=en) in Toulouse and played my first Modern tournament after many years away from competitive Magic. I brought a [Budget Dino Whack](https://www.mtggoldfish.com/articles/budget-magic-100-dino-whack-modern) deck and had pretty modest expectations: learn, have fun and, with a bit of luck, win a game or two. I went 1-7 and finished 134th out of 144 players, but I had a great time and was eager to come back.

So this year I returned to Relic Fest to play the Modern Main Event, which took place yesterday, Sunday, November 1. And things went rather differently!

## Saturday

The main reason I got excited about travelling to Toulouse last year to play in a tournament for a format I had never seriously played was the official playmat illustrated by Rebecca Guay. This year I didn't know the artist ([Donato Giancola](https://donatoarts.com/)) and, to be honest, I wasn't that keen on the illustration (it's the cover of this post). But since we were there, I thought buying the playmat and asking the artist to sign it would make a nice souvenir. That's what we did on the first day, and I have to say Mr. Giancola was incredibly kind!

## Sunday

This time I entered the tournament with [Domain Zoo](https://mtgdecks.net/Modern/domain-aggro). It's an archetype I really like (I also play a version in Pauper), and multicolour decks are generally my favourites. It's obviously much more competitive than last year's deck: it combines very efficient creatures with plenty of interaction and can have explosive starts thanks to the combo of {{< cardname "Leyline of the Guildpact|MKM" >}} and {{< cardname "Scion of Draco" >}}.

{{< combo "Leyline of the Guildpact" "Scion of Draco" "Territorial Kavu" "Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer" "Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd|MH3" >}}

The idea is simple: once we have Domain, we can cast {{< cardname "Scion of Draco" "Scion" >}} for just {{< mana "2" >}}. It gives our creatures several abilities, such as _hexproof_, _lifelink_, _vigilance_, _trample_ or _first strike_ (or all of them if we have {{< cardname "Leyline of the Guildpact|MKM" "Leyline" >}}!). On top of that, {{< cardname "Territorial Kavu" >}} becomes a 5/5 for only {{< mana "{R}{G}" >}}.

The deck also has a more interactive game plan, with {{< cardname "Lightning Bolt|3ED" >}}, {{< cardname "Leyline Binding|DMU" >}} and creatures like {{< cardname "Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd|MH3" >}} letting me answer my opponent's threats while building my own board.

{{< deck id="domain-zoo-relic-fest-2026" >}}

I spent a long time deciding which version of Domain Zoo to play. Lately the more aggressive version seems to be taking over, closer to the deck's original build, with {{< cardname "Wild Nacatl" >}} and {{< cardname "Tribal Flames" >}}. I was also tempted by the version with {{< cardname "Psychic Frog" >}}. But given how things went, I'm very glad I chose the {{< cardname "Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd|MH3" "Phelia" >}} version!

Let the tournament begin!

The second game is much more interesting. My opponent is in control for much of it and manages to play {{< cardname "Leyline of the Guildpact|MKM" "Leyline" >}} on turn four. He also has a {{< cardname "Scion of Draco" "Scion" >}}, which gives his creatures all the abilities it grants.

My creatures gain all the abilities from {{< cardname "Scion of Draco" "Scion" >}}, and I go from defending to having a clearly superior board. My opponent sees it and concedes.

#### Game 3

I adjust my sideboard again for the third game, bringing back some of the cards I had taken out.

The game is close again. We both play two {{< cardname "Territorial Kavu" "Kavu" >}}, but there's an important difference: my opponent gets stuck on two lands and Domain 4. His {{< cardname "Territorial Kavu" "Kavu" >}} are 4/4s while mine are 5/5s. It may not sound like much, but in a mirror match it's huge.

I get {{< cardname "Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd|MH3" "Phelia" >}} and {{< cardname "Quantum Riddler" "Riddler" >}} onto the battlefield. At a decisive moment I cast {{< cardname "Quantum Riddler" "Riddler" >}} with _warp_ and give it _haste_ with {{< cardname "Arena of Glory|MH3" >}}.

I attack with everything and use {{< cardname "Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd|MH3" "Phelia" >}}'s ability to temporarily exile my opponent's {{< cardname "Psychic Frog" "Frog" >}}, removing one of his blockers.

The attack is enough to win.

After the match, DreekC asks what I think of the {{< cardname "Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd|MH3" "Phelia" >}} version of Zoo. It feels strange to be asked, since I'm nowhere near an expert in Modern or this deck, but I tell him it's been working well so far and that I appreciate {{< cardname "Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd|MH3" "Phelia" >}}'s versatility more with every game.

Three rounds, three wins. Things are getting serious!

#### Game 2

The second game isn't much different. I mulligan again, and Thomas controls the game very confidently. Another {{< cardname "Thoughtseize|LRW" "Thoughtseize" >}}, a {{< cardname "Fatal Push" >}} for my {{< cardname "Territorial Kavu" "Kavu" >}}, and a {{< cardname "Prismatic Ending|SOA" >}}...

My first loss of the tournament. My opponent played very well, and I had almost no real chance to win. A good lesson in the gap between knowing the cards in a format and really knowing how to play it.

Still, at 3-1, I'm very happy with the result!

A very unusual game, where a sideboard card combined with {{< cardname "Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd|MH3" "Phelia" >}} proved more decisive than the deck's usual threats.

{{< match round="6" player1="Jordi" deck1="Domain Zoo" deck1Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/2359" player2="yannickgratacos" deck2="Simic Ritual" deck2Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/3705" result="2-0" >}}

In round six I play Yannick, who's on Simic Ritual, a deck I faced last year too.

The game begins with a decent hand, though nothing explosive.

Yannick plays {{< cardname "Harbinger of the Seas|MH3" "Harbinger" >}}. Last year that creature was a death sentence for my deck, and this year it could be a serious problem too, given my Domain Zoo mana base has 20 nonbasic lands and only two basics.

On my next turn I attack with {{< cardname "Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd|MH3" "Phelia" >}} and blink the {{< cardname "Harbinger of the Seas|MH3" "Harbinger" >}}. #### Game 2

I have a hand with {{< cardname "Leyline of the Guildpact|MKM" "Leyline" >}} and {{< cardname "Scion of Draco" "Scion" >}}. One of those hands that looks like it can win all by itself.

Although I could play {{< cardname "Scion of Draco" "Scion" >}} on turn two, I decide not to rush, keeping {{< cardname "Stubborn Denial" "Stubborn Denial" >}} up and waiting until turn three.

When I finally play it, Yannick assesses the situation and concedes immediately, still at 20 life. He has no answer to the creature.

That was a quick win!

5-1. One round to go!

{{< match round="7" player1="Jordi" deck1="Domain Zoo" deck1Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/2359" player2="Martino Bidese" deck2="Rakdos Midrange" deck2Url="https://getpaird.io/tournaments/modern-main-event-8gvZ3V/lists/3695" result="2-1" >}}

I play the final round against a very nice Italian guy named Martino. He's on a Rakdos Midrange list with cards like {{< cardname "Nethergoyf|MH3" >}}, {{< cardname "Fear of Missing Out|DSK" >}} and some of the new cards from Reality Fracture.

{{< combo "Nethergoyf" "Fear of Missing Out" "Moonshadow" "Flare of Malice" "Dark Matter Manipulator" >}}

#### Game 1

We both mulligan: I go to six and he goes to five.

My opponent develops his creatures and I answer with {{< cardname "Lightning Bolt|3ED" "Bolt" >}} and {{< cardname "Leyline Binding|DMU" "Binding" >}}, keeping his board under control.

Meanwhile, I chip away at his life total. It also helps that he deals quite a bit of damage to himself with fetchlands, shocklands and a {{< cardname "Thoughtseize|LRW" "Thoughtseize" >}}.

Between his own damage and my attacks, I win the first game fairly easily, finishing at 20 life.

#### Game 2

Before sideboarding, Martino asks if I'm okay with him calling a judge to clarify an interaction he's unsure about. I say of course, and he gets up to speak to the judge. I only bring in one {{< cardname "Rest in Peace|BIG" "Rest in Peace" >}}.

The second game is much closer and more tense. He plays a new Reality Fracture card I knew from Arena drafts: {{< cardname "Dark Matter Manipulator|FRA" >}}, a one-mana 1/2 black creature that mills three cards when it enters the battlefield and gets +2/+0 for every seven cards in its controller's graveyard.

The creature slowly chips away at me. Thanks to my {{< cardname "Rest in Peace|BIG" "Rest in Peace" >}} on the battlefield, it stays a 1/2 throughout the game, and I hesitate several times over whether to spend a {{< cardname "Lightning Bolt|3ED" "Bolt" >}} on such an apparently harmless creature. I try to answer by attacking with {{< cardname "Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd|MH3" "Phelia" >}}, even though I don't have any especially useful permanents to blink.

The game drags on as we both look for a way to break the stalemate. We remove each other's threats, except for the 1/2. Looking back at my notes of my life total, I see the sequence 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 2... and realize I should have fired the {{< cardname "Lightning Bolt|3ED" "Bolt" >}} I had in hand.

Just when it seems I might still have a chance to win, he finishes me off with a {{< cardname "Lightning Bolt|3ED" "Bolt" >}}.

It all comes down to game three!

#### Game 3

We both mulligan to five and joke that starting with five doesn't feel so bad when we're both in the same boat. The game starts with very few resources and turns into a close battle, with us constantly trading creatures and answers.

He gets stuck on two lands for a few turns and I don't have Domain yet, but I cast two {{< cardname "Leyline Binding|DMU" "Binding" >}} for {{< mana "{1}{W}" >}} to exile a {{< cardname "Fear of Missing Out|DSK" "FOMO" >}} and a {{< cardname "Moonshadow" >}}.

At one point I have {{< cardname "Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd|MH3" "Phelia" >}} and {{< cardname "Teferi, Time Raveler|WAR" "Teferi" >}} on the battlefield. My opponent has a {{< cardname "Moonshadow" "Moonshadow" >}} that I want to remove with {{< cardname "Prismatic Ending|SOA" "Ending" >}}. He responds with {{< cardname "Flare of Malice" >}}, sacrificing {{< cardname "Moonshadow" "Moonshadow" >}} to remove my {{< cardname "Teferi, Time Raveler|WAR" "Teferi" >}}. We resolve the play, I attack with {{< cardname "Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd|MH3" "Phelia" >}}, and pass the turn. He draws a card and finally plays his third land. That's when I see my {{< cardname "Teferi, Time Raveler|WAR" "Teferi" >}} in the graveyard and realize my mistake: with {{< cardname "Teferi, Time Raveler|WAR" "Teferi" >}} on the battlefield, my opponent couldn't cast an instant in response to {{< cardname "Prismatic Ending|SOA" "Ending" >}}!

We call a judge. Since the game has moved on and my opponent has drawn a card, fixing it isn't as simple as undoing the last action.

The judges eventually decide to perform a rollback. One of the five cards in my opponent's hand is chosen at random and put on top of his library. We return to the moment {{< cardname "Prismatic Ending|SOA" "Ending" >}} was on the stack, before the illegal response.

We both receive warnings: he for casting an instant when he couldn't, and me for failing to maintain the game state properly and allowing the error to happen.

But the game goes on.

Martino, who had some mana trouble in the early turns, manages to play a {{< cardname "Blood Moon|MMA" >}}.

Fortunately, I have a basic Plains, which lets me keep casting {{< cardname "Phelia, Exuberant Shepherd|MH3" "Phelia" >}}. I use her ability to exile {{< cardname "Blood Moon|MMA" "Blood Moon" >}} temporarily several times, restoring access to the colours of mana I need, at least during my second main phase each turn.

Then comes one of the most interesting plays of the whole tournament.
